\subsection{Expanded systematic extraction within the defined corpus}
\label{sec:systematic-extraction}
The expansion performed on 5 October 2026 changes the depth of inspection, not
the declared September literature-discovery cutoff. The unit of inclusion remains
the 50 distinct works in the original identifier manifest. All 50 receive the
same structured appraisal fields; 46 have selected primary full-text passages
inspected, while four retain explicitly limited evidence. A source being available
as a PDF is not counted as an independently replicated result or as proof that
every appendix has been read.

This is a \textbf{systematic evidence review of a defined, purposively assembled
corpus}, with thematic synthesis. It is not an exhaustive systematic search of
the entire robotics literature. The original discovery process did not retain a
complete export of all database hits, and there was no independent dual screening.
Those facts cannot be repaired by retrospectively inventing a PRISMA count or
calling an automated download a second reviewer. The systematic contribution of
this expansion is the explicit extraction instrument, source-level access record,
consistent critical questions and traceable synthesis across every retained work.

\subsection{Retrieval, version control and access limitations}
Existing arXiv identifiers and corresponding-preprint locators were combined with
DOI-matched OpenAlex location records, institutional repositories and author
copies. OpenAlex was used to locate documents, not as the source of experimental
results. Accessible PDFs were cached locally, converted to text and inspected
at the loci recorded in \repo{paper_appraisals.json}. URLs, timestamps, failed
attempts and content hashes are retained in \repo{fulltext_retrieval.json}.
The copyrighted primary PDFs remain in the ignored local build cache; the
delivered PDF contains original analysis and links, not a redistributed bundle
of the papers.

An author manuscript and a publisher version can differ. For example, the
retrieved DS-RNN text identifies a later arXiv revision than the conference year,
while the Parkour manuscript predates its journal publication. Social Force also
has a later repository deposit than its original journal date. The bibliography
continues to identify the selected work; the appraisal identifies the inspected
copy. Fine-grained claims belong to that copy until checked against the final
version. Unversioned repository URLs are additionally fingerprinted by their
retrieved PDF hash.

The original full texts that remain unavailable in this pass are MuJoCo (2012),
the 2013 sparse timed-trajectory paper, the 1998 velocity-obstacle paper and the
2013 dense-crowd cooperation paper. Their identities are verified. Available
abstracts, institutional records and author software descriptions support bounded
context, but they are not silently substituted for original methods and results.
In particular, no new table-level quantitative claim is extracted from those
unavailable texts. The four limitations are visible in the appraisals and audit.

\subsection{Extraction instrument and thematic coding}
Every paper is appraised using the following fields:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Problem and contribution:} what question it addresses and which
part of the autonomy pipeline it changes.
\item \textbf{Mechanism:} representation, policy/planner structure, information
inputs, action interface and important modeling assumptions.
\item \textbf{Evidence:} the inspected study type, scenario, comparator,
measurement boundary and reported result where directly supported.
\item \textbf{Critical appraisal:} limitations in internal validity,
comparability, deployment relevance or interpretation; these are identified as
the review's analysis rather than attributed wholesale to the authors.
\item \textbf{Novelty implication:} what the paper rules out as new, and which
more specific experiment or contribution it motivates.
\item \textbf{Source anchor:} inspected sections/tables, source copy and reading
level, so the claim can be traced back to material rather than a citation count.
\end{enumerate}
The appraisals are grouped into eight analytical themes: social requirements;
geometric interaction and safety; learned interaction/prediction; perception and
estimation; datasets/evaluation; locomotion/transfer; sensor-level humanoid
navigation; and enabling systems. These analytical themes overlap in substance:
the grouping is a reading order, not a claim that each method belongs to only one
scientific category. The separate web resource index uses a different set of
navigation-oriented clusters for discoverability.

The synthesis asks the same cross-cutting questions across themes. Is an agent
state observed, inferred or privileged? Does the action mean velocity, trajectory,
joint target or torque? Is the crowd reciprocal, replayed or independent? Does
``collision'' mean contact, body overlap or a distance intrusion? Does runtime
measure inference, an entire callback or the age of the observation at actuation?
Are real people providing outcome judgments, or are geometric proxies being used?
These distinctions make apparently similar success numbers scientifically
nonexchangeable.

\subsection{Quality appraisal without a misleading total score}
The review does not assign a synthetic ``quality score'' to dissimilar papers.
A theorem, a dataset, a taxonomy and a hardware deployment supply different
forms of evidence. Instead, six appraisal dimensions are applied where relevant:
\begin{description}
\item[Source validity.] Are sensing inputs, privileged training inputs and
evaluation truth identifiable and separated?
\item[Comparator validity.] Do baselines share task conditions, tuning budget,
observations and unavoidable actuation limits? Are adapters disclosed?
\item[Execution validity.] Is the claimed motion physically executed, and are
its dynamics and contact model appropriate to the claim?
\item[Temporal validity.] Are acquisition time, processing delay and applied
command identity distinguishable?
\item[Statistical validity.] Are exposure, denominators, repeats, uncertainty
and failed/invalid trials described sufficiently for the claim?
\item[Construct and transfer validity.] Does the metric measure the stated
construct, and what changes between simulation, hardware and human evaluation?
\end{description}
``Not established by inspected material'' is not equivalent to ``absent from the
entire paper or codebase''. This wording is particularly important for supplements,
private evaluation logs and newer revisions that were not inspected. Likewise,
an author's empirical success is not discredited simply because a different
application needs stronger evidence; the review identifies the transfer boundary.

\subsection{Synthesis rules and strength of gap claims}
No meta-analysis of success rates is attempted. The corpus mixes different
robots, information sources, trial lengths, task definitions and risk metrics.
Quantitative values are used to explain a method's own evidence and its limits,
not to form a cross-paper leaderboard. Thematic conclusions are instead built
from converging mechanisms, explicit counterexamples and repeated methodological
issues. For example, DWA and the biped MPC paper invalidate broad claims that
dynamics or stopping were ignored; SCAND invalidates a claim that human judgments
are absent from social-navigation evaluation \citep{dwa,biped2016,scand}.

A gap is described at one of three levels: a \emph{demonstrated project deficit},
such as recorded contact failures; an \emph{under-tested interface in this corpus},
such as calibrated observation-age/execution coupling; or a \emph{candidate
research contribution} requiring a more extensive nearest-neighbor literature
search and a new mechanism. These levels must not be collapsed. The bounded
corpus does not establish the absence of every delayed-CBF, robust/chance-constrained
MPC, conformal-calibration or contact-aware manipulation method. Those literatures
are explicit pre-submission search priorities.

The extraction and synthesis were prepared in a single AI-assisted review
workflow, without independent second-person adjudication. The delivered structured
records make such review possible but do not replace it. No literature-review
protocol was preregistered, and no new physical trial was performed. These
limitations define the strength of the conclusions while preserving a substantial
increase in primary-material analysis over the previous abstract-led report.
